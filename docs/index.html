Surprisal Theory Is Tautological
arXiv · ●●●○○ Significant
›

Surprisal theory claims human processing difficulty is an affine function of surprisal under a language model, but this claim is a tautology without further constraints. The theory makes no falsifiable predictions without an additional constraint on the language model.

This matters for language models because it challenges the fundamental assumptions of surprisal theory, which has implications for how we evaluate and improve language model performance.

LLMs Learn Moral Reasoning
arXiv · ●●●○○ Significant
›

Researchers studied how large language models update their moral judgments based on social influence, finding that models are more receptive to nearby positions and influenced by views presented as their own prior judgments. The study reveals a broader judgment-updating process shaped by social influence, recasting sycophancy as one aspect of this process.

This research matters because it helps developers create more socially calibrated language models that can learn from others without simply yielding to them, leading to more nuanced and well-grounded moral reasoning in AI systems.
